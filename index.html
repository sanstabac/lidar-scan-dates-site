How this works

Lidar is a laser scanner flown on a plane. It fires hundreds of thousands of pulses per second and records where each one bounces back, building up millions of 3D points. The plane flies back and forth in straight sweeps called flight passes, and a big area takes many days to cover.

Every point in the data carries a GPS timestamp of the moment its pulse was fired. This page reads those timestamps and converts them to Pacific time. Times for a square are the typical time for that pass within the square. Times for a single laser point are exact to the millisecond.

Zoomed out, colors show the main scan day for each 30 × 30 ft area. Zoomed in, colors and the side panel use 6 × 6 ft squares and every point. Overlapping passes are normal: survey crews overlap passes to fill gaps and check accuracy.

Source: Washington DNR Lidar Portal, project "Cascades North Wali 2023". The project name says 2023, but the timestamps show when each area was actually flown.
